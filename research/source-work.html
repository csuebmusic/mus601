Source work

One edition, two sources, five times over

Every analytical paper carries the same source requirement: the authoritative edition of each work you analyze, plus two secondary sources, each of the two annotated. The quantity holds all term. What changes is how much of the finding is done for you, which research tool comes into play, which citation form you handle, and which test of trustworthiness is the explicit lens.

Every citation form named on this page is set out in the Chicago guide. This page is what to do and when; that one is how to write it down.

Annotations go in a separate document, outside the paper's six-to-eight-page count: stapled behind the paper for Papers 1 through 4, uploaded alongside it for Paper 5. The edition and the two secondary sources also appear in the paper's footnotes and bibliography.

the constant requirement

  • Cite the authoritative edition of every work you analyze. The count follows the number of works: three editions for Paper 1, two for Papers 2 and 3, one for Papers 4 and 5.
  • Find two secondary sources that bear on your analysis, and annotate both.
  • Secondary sources are scholarly books, journal articles, dissertations, and reference entries. Program notes, blog posts, and textbook summaries do not count toward the two.

Which edition you cite is a scholarly decision. An IMSLP scan is often a public-domain reprint or a performance edition rather than the modern critical edition a scholar would cite, and the difference shows in an accidental, a slur, or a dynamic.

judging a source

Five questions, applied to everything you cite.

  • Peer review. Was the work vetted by other scholars before publication, and by what standard? A dissertation is committee-vetted, which is a different thing from peer-reviewed, and worth naming as such.
  • Author standing. Who wrote it, what else have they written, and where does their work sit in the literature on this repertoire?
  • Venue. A university press, a refereed journal, a major reference work, and a concert program carry different weight, set by who checked the claims.
  • Currency. How old is it, and does age matter here? A 1985 article can still be the authority on its subject. Decide which case you are in, and be prepared to argue it.
  • Edition and recording authority. For scores and recordings, what makes this text or this performance the one to cite?

the annotated entry

An annotation runs 100 to 150 words and does three things: summarize the source, judge it, and say what it is good for in your paper. Head each entry with the full Chicago bibliography citation, then the paragraph.

For Paper 1, use this structure directly:

Summary. In one or two sentences, what does this source argue or establish? Name the claim, not the topic.

Judgment. Which of the five tests apply here, and how does the source come out on them? Where is it strong, and where would you hold it at arm's length?

Use. What does this source do for your analysis specifically? Point at the passage, the movement, or the analytical problem it bears on.

From Paper 2 onward, write the paragraph as continuous prose rather than under the three labels. The three jobs stay the same.
