reading the board

The top line gives the littera, the letter name of each note. Below it each hexachord has a line of its own, labelled by the note carrying its ut and by its species, with ut re mi fa sol la written under the six notes it covers. Read down a column and you get that note's voces: G is sol in the naturale on C, re in the molle on F, and ut in the durum on G, which is the composite name G sol re ut. Click a note to light its column.

The mode bar takes the accent, washed under the species of fifth and outlined under the species of fourth, with the final marked below. The hexachord bars are blue. The tetrachord bars are dashed and set in the muted grey.

A note on one collision of names. The Guidonian gamut divides into registers called graves, acutae, and superacutae, printed above the letters. The South German tetrachords carry the functional names of the Enchiriadis tradition, graves, finales, superiores, and excellentes. The word graves names two different spans: the register from Γ to G, and the tetrachord from A to D.

the three layers

  • hexachords, scalar. Seven overlapping deductiones in three species: durum with hard b as mi, naturale with neither b, molle with soft b as fa. Each is a T–S–T tetrachord with a whole tone at each end, the largest scale segment whose series of tones and semitones replicates at intervals other than the octave, at the fourth and the fifth. The semitone always falls between mi and fa. This is the module for solmization and position finding. From Guido of Arezzo and the North Italian school, his four treatises c. 1026 to 1033.
  • tetrachords, scalar. Four T–S–T tetrachords carrying the functional names of the Enchiriadis tradition: graves A–D, finales D–G, superiores a–d, excellentes d–g. From the South German school: Berno of Reichenau's Prologus in tonarium, after 1021, and Hermannus Contractus's Musica, c. 1050. The scale beneath them is Hucbald of Saint-Amand's, from his Musica of c. 900, and the names come from the Enchiriadis treatises of the late ninth century.
  • modes, modal. Eight octaves around four finals, D, E, F, and G, which are the four notes of the finales tetrachord. Each octave divides at a species of fifth and a species of fourth, the fourth above the fifth in an authentic mode and below it in a plagal. From the same school, in the interpolated chapters of the Prologus.

The board is a composite. Cohen sets these out as the doctrines of two schools, the North Italian and the South German, distinct and ultimately compatible. The pitch field drawn here is the Italian one, Γ to ee. The South German writers work on Hucbald's two-octave scale, A to aa, and Berno's series of species begins at A. Hermannus takes the low gamma from the Italian school later. The layers are also not contemporaries: Hucbald's scale and the Enchiriadis names reach the eleventh century as inheritance from the ninth.

Select a mode and the readout names the hexachords its octave crosses and the notes they share. A hexachord spans six notes and a modal octave eight, so no modal octave lies inside a single hexachord. Singing through one requires mutation: at a note carrying two or three voces, the singer treats it as a pivot and shifts from one vox to another, moving from a higher vox in one hexachord to a lower one in another to continue upward, and the reverse to continue downward.

Mutation is not permitted between b fa and b mi. In the upper two octaves those two voces stand for different pitches, soft b and hard b, so the readout leaves the b out of the notes it offers as pivots.
