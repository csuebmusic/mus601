Three divisions of one gamut

The Guidonian gamut cut three ways: into hexachords for solmization, into modal octaves around a final, and into the tetrachords of the South German school. The three partitions run over the same pitch field and do not line up.

reading the board

The littera is the letter name of the note and the voces beneath it are its solmization syllables, one for each hexachord the note belongs to. Most notes carry two or three, since the seven hexachords overlap. Click a note to light the hexachords that contain it and the syllable it takes in each.

The mode bar takes the accent, washed under the species of fifth and outlined under the species of fourth, with the final marked below. The hexachord bars are blue. The tetrachord bars are dashed and set in the muted grey.

A note on one collision of names. The Guidonian gamut divides into registers called graves, acutae, and superacutae, printed above the letters. The South German tetrachords carry the functional names of the Enchiriadis tradition, graves, finales, superiores, and excellentes. The word graves names two different spans: the register from Γ to G, and the tetrachord from A to D.

what the layers show

The hexachord is a T–S–T tetrachord with a whole tone at each end, placed at its seven possible positions in three species: durum with hard b as mi, naturale with neither b, molle with soft b as fa. It's the largest scale segment whose internal series of tones and semitones replicates at intervals other than the octave, at the fourth and the fifth. Its boundaries fall where they do to keep the semitone between mi and fa.

The modal octave divides at the final into a species of fifth and a species of fourth. The four finals, D, E, F, and G, are the four notes of the finales tetrachord. An authentic mode sets the fourth above the fifth, a plagal mode below.

Select a mode and the readout names the hexachords its octave crosses and the notes they share. No modal octave lies inside a single hexachord, so singing one through requires mutation at a shared note.

The species and the modal octaves are set out in the modal species handout.
