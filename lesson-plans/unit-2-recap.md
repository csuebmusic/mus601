# unit 2: paper 2 recap

This is the lesson plan for the Paper 2 meeting on October 13, in the order of the meeting: the context and the system, then each work with its ritornello and form charts, then the comparison.

## context

None of the unit 2 readings analyzes either concerto. The nearest model for the systems is Burnett and Nitzberg's first movement of Vivaldi's Op. 9 no. 2, also in A major (pp. 145–51), and for Bach's plan their coda on the first movement of Brandenburg 2 (pp. 152–53). Dreyfus gives the segmentation by function and the bracketed segment lists used in the charts (pp. 330–38).

Vivaldi wrote eight concertos for viola d'amore, and the Pietà's records reimburse him for viola d'amore strings in 1708 and 1709. RV 396 is the only one whose solo part is notated in alto clef, read an octave higher than written (Cedille Records, notes to *The Complete Viola d'Amore Concertos*; web source, not the readings).

Bach's autograph of BWV 1055, D-B Mus.ms. Bach P 234, dates from about 1738. Three writers have proposed a lost original for a melody instrument: Donald Francis Tovey the oboe d'amore (1935), Wilhelm Mohr the viola d'amore (1972), and Pieter Dirksen argued against the viola d'amore (2008) (Wikipedia, "Harpsichord Concerto in A major, BWV 1055"; web source, not the readings).

## the system

Both movements are in the 3♯ system, root A. For Paper 2 the system analysis is reference for papers that track it. The tonal system rules and the arithmetic for every unit 2 key are in `unit-2-readings.md`, under the Paper 2 directed questions.

- The central hexachord in fifths is D–A–E–b–f♯–c♯, IV I V ii vi iii. G♯, the leading tone, is in the system.
- Also in the system are the raised thirds of the applied dominants, D♯ (V/V), A♯ (V/ii), and E♯ (V/vi), and the allowable flat, G♮ (V7/IV).
- The twelfth pitch class is the missing pitch, in two spellings. C♮, the minor third of A, brings the parallel minor and shifts the system three signatures flatward to 0, root C; D♯ corrects it. B♯, the leading tone of iii, shifts it three signatures sharpward to 6♯, root F♯; A♮ corrects it. C♯ minor belongs to the 6♯ system (Burnett pp. 118, 146–47, Figure 4.1).
- The hexachord pairs E with c♯, A with f♯, and D with b, each pair sharing an implied signature. In Op. 9 no. 2, D♯ stays active from the ritornello in V to the ritornello in iii (Burnett p. 148).

## comparison

The structural plan is the same in both movements, I–V–iii–I.

- Between V and iii, Bach's ritornellos climb the hexachord by fifths, E–b–f♯–c♯, so r4 in ii and r5 in vi are passing areas. Burnett describes the same climb in the first movement of Brandenburg 2, B♭–F–c–g–d–a, where the minor dominant is "without structural significance" (p. 153). Vivaldi moves from V to iii within solo 2, the order of Op. 9 no. 2 (Burnett p. 148).
- From iii, Vivaldi returns to I at once, in the solo interruption at m. 51. Bach's solo 6 descends the fifths from c♯ to A over twelve bars.
- Vivaldi uses both spellings of the missing pitch: C♮ in the pianoidée (0 system) and B♯ around iii (6♯). Bach uses B♯ only.
- Vivaldi's solos restate ritornello motives, and the tutti and solo scoring marks the sections. No solo in Bach states the ritornello theme: the ritornello material is in the ritornellos only, and its presence and absence marks the sections (Dreyfus pp. 334, 339). Bach's solos run 6 or 8 bars, and each two-bar r completes an 8-bar unit; solo 6 with r6 and r7 makes 16.
- Vivaldi builds each return from a different selection of segments: [V′ F′ E′], [P′], [F′], [F P E]. In V the pianoidée's motive becomes the Fortspinnung, in the major; the pianoidée itself returns only in iii and in the tonic. The Vordersatz returns only in V; in the tonic, its opening motive returns only inside the Epilog. Bach's internal returns are the two-bar Vordersatz alone, except R3, part 1 in V, and the double theme returns intact only at the close.
- Dreyfus holds that key order is "anachronistically imposed by formal models derived from later eighteenth-century music" (p. 334) and stresses "the essential lack of an assigned harmonic plan prefiguring the tonal ARRAY" (p. 355). The shared plan is presented with that objection beside it.
