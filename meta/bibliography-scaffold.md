# MUS 601: research and bibliography scaffold

Peter's response to `bibliography-module-brief.md`. Canonical for the research and bibliography arc in 601: the per-paper source task, the tool sequencing, the trustworthiness lens, and the scaffolding level all come from here. Typography normalized to the repo's style (em dashes removed); wording is Peter's.

---

Ines, your brief is explicit about volume: you'd rather students get one score citation and two solid secondary sources exactly right, five times, rather than assemble a long list they can't evaluate. That's the constraint this plan takes literally. Every paper below carries the same footprint: one authoritative edition citation, two secondary sources, both annotated. Nothing scales up in quantity across the term.

What does scale is everything else: how much of the source-finding is done for them versus by them, which research tool is newly in play, which Chicago citation type is being practiced, and which trustworthiness criterion is the explicit lens for that paper. By Paper 5, a student has independently exercised every item on your 601 exit list at least once, and most of them several times, at a constant, evaluable scale. That's also what should let 602 open directly onto the annotated-bibliography arc without re-teaching the floor.

## paper-by-paper

### paper 1: Lasso, Monteverdi, Schütz (week 6)

**Repertoire.** *Lagrime di San Pietro* I and V; "Perché te'n fuggi, o Fillide?"; "Fili mi, Absalon."

**New this paper.** First real RILM search (guided, in class or via a short worksheet); and the distinction between a score's availability and its authority: IMSLP often carries a public-domain or performance edition, not the modern critical edition a scholar would cite.

**The source task.** One edition citation per work (three total, since this is a three-way comparison), plus two secondary sources. One secondary source may be drawn directly from the Unit 1 bibliography you already assigned (e.g. Powers 1981, or the relevant Christensen chapter). Students still have to cite and annotate it correctly, they just aren't finding it blind. The second is their first solo RILM search.

**Citation types practiced.** Score/critical edition; chapter in an edited volume.

**Trustworthiness lens.** Edition/recording authority (why this edition and not another) and publisher venue (a Cambridge UP anthology versus, say, program notes).

**Annotation.** Templated using a fill-in-the-blank structure (summary / judgment / what it's good for) so the first attempt isn't also the first time they've seen the form.

**Scaffolding level.** Heavy. This paper is training wheels for the mechanics; the analytical work in the paper itself carries the real weight.

### paper 2: Vivaldi RV 396 / Bach BWV 1055 (week 9)

**Repertoire.** First movements of both A-major concertos.

**New this paper.** Independent JSTOR search; WorldCat to locate the specific edition volume (e.g., the relevant NBA volume for Bach).

**The source task.** One edition citation per concerto, plus two independently located secondary sources. The assigned Dreyfus article can anchor one; the second must be found, not supplied.

**Citation types practiced.** Journal article (new); reinforce score/edition.

**Trustworthiness lens.** Peer review, and currency: is a 1985 article still the authority on ritornello form, and how would a student argue that either way? Also a nice contrast case: Bach's editorial tradition (NBA) is far more standardized than Vivaldi's, so the two edition citations aren't equally straightforward to defend.

**Annotation.** Free-form now but with the same three-part structure, no template.

**Scaffolding level.** Guided. A short source-finding log (what you searched, what you rejected and why) turned in a few days ahead of the paper, or a brief check-in, keeps the independence real without letting anyone drift for two weeks before finding out their sources don't hold up.

### paper 3: Mozart "Jupiter" / Haydn Symphony No. 90 (week 12)

**Repertoire.** First movements of both symphonies.

**New this paper.** Grove / Oxford Music Online; footnote-chasing, i.e., following a citation out of an assigned reading (Hepokoski is the natural anchor here) to a further source, rather than starting a search from scratch.

**The source task.** One edition citation per symphony, plus two secondary sources, at least one of which was located by chasing a footnote rather than a direct database search.

**Citation types practiced.** Online reference-work entry (new); reinforce journal article.

**Trustworthiness lens.** Author standing, why a named Sonata Theory scholar's claim carries particular weight in this argument, alongside the editorial vetting behind a Grove entry.

**Annotation.** Free-form, and peer-checked: students trade source picks with a classmate before submitting, a low-stakes sanity check rather than a graded step.

**Scaffolding level.** Guided-to-independent. The safety net is a peer, not an instructor or a template.

### paper 4: Beethoven, "Kreutzer" Sonata (week 14)

**Repertoire.** First movement. This is the first single-work paper, which changes the shape of the source task.

**New this paper.** ProQuest Dissertations; and, if the analysis engages a specific recorded performance, a recording/liner-notes authority judgment.

**The source task.** One edition citation, defended rather than just stated. Beethoven has real competing editions (Henle Urtext against older complete-works editions), so the student has to argue for the one they're using. Plus two secondary sources, at least one found independently via ProQuest or JSTOR with no assigned reading to anchor it.

**Citation types practiced.** Dissertation entry and/or liner notes (new); reinforce all prior types.

**Trustworthiness lens.** Recording/edition authority in full: a short justification paragraph (why this edition, why this recording if one is used), plus the distinction between committee-vetted (dissertation) and peer-reviewed (journal) standards.

**Annotation.** Independent, no scaffold.

**Scaffolding level.** Independent. This is the dress rehearsal for Paper 5.

### paper 5: Schubert, String Quartet No. 14 (finals week)

**Repertoire.** First movement, the capstone paper for the sequence.

**New this paper.** None. No new tool is introduced; the ask is full, unscaffolded integration of everything above. One light preview of a 602 skill: the two Webster articles on Schubert's sonata form make a natural pair to read against each other, which previews, in miniature, the source-synthesis 602 will ask for at length. That's a preview, not a 602-level demand.

**The source task.** One edition citation, plus two independently located and evaluated secondary sources, fully annotated, with no assigned reading, template, or peer check as a backstop.

**Citation types practiced.** Whichever types the sources actually require. The test is that the student picks the right form without being told which one applies.

**Trustworthiness lens.** All five criteria, applied without prompting.

**Annotation.** Independent and fluent.

**Scaffolding level.** None. This paper is the readiness gate: whatever a student can't yet do unaided here is exactly what 602's annotated bibliography will otherwise have to re-teach in Week 1.

*One thing I'd like your take on, Ines: whether this paper's bibliography component should function explicitly as a checkpoint, i.e., feedback tied point-by-point to the 601 outcomes, the way the MUS 202 midterm conference works as an authenticity gate, or simply stand as the last paper in the sequence with no special status attached.*

## where this leaves them for 602

Every item on your 601 exit list has been exercised independently at least once by Paper 5, most of them three or four times, always at the same one-edition-plus-two-source scale. The 602 annotated bibliography (eight to twelve sources) is a scale jump, not a skill jump. Students arrive already knowing how to find, judge, cite, and annotate a source; 602 just asks them to do more of it, in conversation with itself, toward a paper rather than a close reading.

## open questions for you

- Confirm the constant-volume premise (one edition citation and two secondary sources on all five papers) is what you want held fixed, rather than letting the number of sources grow across the term.

- Whether Paper 1's training-wheel move (one of the two secondary sources pre-identified from the assigned bibliography) is acceptable, or whether you'd rather all five papers require fully independent source-finding from day one.

- Whether the source/annotation work is graded inside the paper grade, or as a small separate companion grade (a short annotated bibliography submitted alongside each paper). This decides whether it needs its own line in the rubric you mentioned is still in development.

- Confirm (or reshuffle) the tool sequencing itself: RILM to Paper 1, JSTOR/WorldCat to Paper 2, Grove/footnote-chasing to Paper 3, ProQuest Dissertations to Paper 4. You named this toolkit as a whole in your brief, so I distributed it across the papers, and it's worth checking the order suits you. Relatedly, do Grove/footnote-chasing and ProQuest need their own short Canvas mini-lessons, or can they fold into that week's existing lecture?

- Whether Paper 5's bibliography component should carry explicit checkpoint status feeding into 602, as noted above.

## resolutions

Answered, and reflected in the student-facing files:

- Constant volume, confirmed. Stated to students as the authoritative edition for each work analyzed, plus two secondary sources, since the edition count follows the number of works (three on Paper 1, two on Papers 2 and 3, one on Papers 4 and 5). The two secondary sources are the fixed quantity.
- Paper 1's pre-identified source, confirmed.
- Tool sequencing, confirmed as proposed.
- Grading: inside the paper score. The annotations are submitted as a separate attachment and sit outside the 6-to-8-page count, so they do not take space from the analysis. `syllabus.md` names the source work in what earns the score rather than adding a sixth graded component.
- Paper 5 checkpoint status: not yet settled. Student-facing text currently gives Paper 5 no special status. Adding the checkpoint reading is a change to the feedback structure only, not to the assignment.
- Canvas mini-lessons for Grove/footnote-chasing and ProQuest: still Peter's call to make with the module.
