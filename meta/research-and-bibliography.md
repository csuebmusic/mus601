# research and bibliography

Canonical for the research and bibliography work in 601: where students start, what they can do by the end, and the per-paper arc that gets them there. `research/source-work.md` is the student-facing version of the paper-by-paper section, and `research/chicago-notes-bibliography.md` carries the citation forms. Keep both in step with this file.

Research and bibliography used to be its own course. It runs now as one module inside 601 and a second inside 602, built by Peter in Canvas against the outcomes below.

## where students start

Assume no prior research training at the start of 601. Students cannot yet find scholarly sources, tell a strong one from a weak one, or cite anything correctly. Many have never opened RILM or a critical edition, and cannot tell a primary source from a secondary one. 601 builds from that floor, and 602 assumes it rather than teaching it again.

## the 601 exit list

By the end of 601 a student can:

- Tell primary sources (scores and critical editions, composer writings and letters, contemporaneous documents, recordings) from secondary ones (scholarly books, journal articles, dissertations, reference entries), and scholarly sources from everything else.
- Judge what makes a source trustworthy: peer review, the author's standing, the venue or publisher, currency, and, specific to music, whether an edition or a recording is authoritative. Which edition you cite is a scholarly decision.
- Find sources with the tools of the field: RILM, JSTOR, Oxford Music Online and Grove, the library catalog and WorldCat, ProQuest Dissertations, and IMSLP and critical editions for scores. Move from a topic to search terms, and follow the footnotes of a good source to further sources.
- Cite in Chicago, notes-bibliography: footnotes and bibliography entries for the source types the seminar uses, including scores and editions, book chapters, journal articles, dissertations, liner notes, and online reference works.
- Write an annotated entry that summarizes the source, judges it, and says what it is good for.

## the shape of the arc

Every paper carries the same footprint: the authoritative edition of each work analyzed, plus two secondary sources, both annotated. Nothing scales up in quantity across the term. The volume stays small on purpose, since a student who gets an edition citation and two solid sources exactly right five times has a skill that a long unevaluated list does not build.

What scales is everything else: how much of the source-finding is done for students rather than by them, which research tool is newly in play, which Chicago citation type is being practiced, and which trustworthiness criterion is the explicit lens. By Paper 5 a student has independently exercised every item on the exit list at least once, most of them several times, at a constant, evaluable scale.

## paper-by-paper

Each tool is introduced in the seminar meeting of the week it comes due, folded into that week's lecture rather than carried by a separate Canvas mini-lesson.

### paper 1: Lasso, Monteverdi, Schütz (week 6)

**Repertoire.** *Lagrime di San Pietro* I and V; "Perché te'n fuggi, o Fillide?"; "Fili mi, Absalon."

**New this paper.** First real RILM search, guided, in class or via a short worksheet. Also the distinction between a score's availability and its authority: IMSLP often carries a public-domain or performance edition rather than the modern critical edition a scholar would cite.

**The source task.** One edition citation per work, three in all, since this is a three-way comparison, plus two secondary sources. One secondary source may be drawn from the Unit 1 bibliography already assigned, Powers 1981 or the relevant Christensen chapter among them. Students still cite and annotate it correctly, they simply are not finding it blind. The second is their first solo RILM search.

**Citation types practiced.** Score or critical edition; chapter in an edited volume.

**Trustworthiness lens.** Edition and recording authority (why this edition rather than another) and publisher venue (a Cambridge UP anthology against program notes).

**Annotation.** Templated, fill-in-the-blank (summary / judgment / use), so the first attempt is not also the first sight of the form.

**Scaffolding level.** Heavy. This paper trains the mechanics; the analytical work carries the real weight.

### paper 2: Vivaldi RV 396 / Bach BWV 1055 (week 9)

**Repertoire.** First movements of both A-major concertos.

**New this paper.** Independent JSTOR search; WorldCat to locate the specific edition volume, the relevant NBA volume for the Bach.

**The source task.** One edition citation per concerto, plus two independently located secondary sources. The assigned Dreyfus article can anchor one; the second must be found.

**Citation types practiced.** Journal article; reinforce score and edition.

**Trustworthiness lens.** Peer review and currency: is a 1985 article still the authority on ritornello form, and how would a student argue it either way? The pair also makes a contrast case, since Bach's editorial tradition is far more standardized than Vivaldi's, so the two edition citations are not equally straightforward to defend.

**Annotation.** Free-form, same three parts, no template.

**Scaffolding level.** Guided. A short source-finding log (what was searched, what was rejected and why) comes in a few days ahead of the paper, so nobody drifts for two weeks before finding out their sources do not hold up.

### paper 3: Mozart "Jupiter" / Haydn Symphony No. 90 (week 12)

**Repertoire.** First movements of both symphonies.

**New this paper.** Grove and Oxford Music Online; footnote-chasing, following a citation out of an assigned reading, Hepokoski being the natural anchor, rather than starting a search from scratch.

**The source task.** One edition citation per symphony, plus two secondary sources, at least one located by chasing a footnote rather than by direct database search.

**Citation types practiced.** Online reference-work entry; reinforce journal article.

**Trustworthiness lens.** Author standing, why a named Sonata Theory scholar's claim carries particular weight in this argument, alongside the editorial vetting behind a Grove entry.

**Annotation.** Free-form and peer-checked: students trade source picks with a classmate before submitting, a low-stakes sanity check rather than a graded step.

**Scaffolding level.** Guided to independent. The safety net is a peer rather than an instructor or a template.

### paper 4: Beethoven, "Kreutzer" Sonata (week 14)

**Repertoire.** First movement. The first single-work paper, which changes the shape of the source task.

**New this paper.** ProQuest Dissertations, and, where the analysis engages a specific recorded performance, a recording and liner-notes authority judgment.

**The source task.** One edition citation, defended rather than stated. Beethoven has genuinely competing editions, Henle Urtext against the older complete-works editions, so the student argues for the one in use. Plus two secondary sources, at least one located independently through ProQuest or JSTOR.

**Citation types practiced.** Dissertation, and liner notes where relevant; reinforce all prior types.

**Trustworthiness lens.** Edition and recording authority in full: a short justification paragraph (why this edition, why this recording if one is used), plus the difference between committee-vetted and peer-reviewed standards.

**Annotation.** Independent, no scaffold.

**Scaffolding level.** Independent. The dress rehearsal for Paper 5.

### paper 5: Schubert, String Quartet No. 14 (finals week)

**Repertoire.** First movement, the capstone of the sequence.

**New this paper.** No new tool. The ask is full, unscaffolded integration of everything above. One light preview of a 602 skill: the two Webster articles on Schubert's sonata form make a natural pair to read against each other, which previews the source synthesis 602 asks for at length.

**The source task.** One edition citation, plus two independently located and evaluated secondary sources, fully annotated, with no assigned reading, template, or peer check as a backstop.

**Citation types practiced.** Whichever the sources require. The test is that the student picks the right form without being told which applies.

**Trustworthiness lens.** All five criteria, applied without prompting.

**Annotation.** Independent and fluent.

**Scaffolding level.** None.

**Checkpoint.** Paper 5's source component is the readiness gate into 602. Feedback comes back keyed point by point to the exit list, so what a student cannot yet do unaided is named before the spring rather than discovered in 602's first week.

## into 602

The papers drive the difference between the two modules. In 601 the papers are short and analytical, close readings that hold historical and interpretive context to a brief frame, so the bibliography is lean and in service of the analysis. In 602 the work culminates in a full research paper developed and revised across the term, where the scholarly context is the point and the bibliography is the backbone.

The 602 module runs alongside that paper sequence: research question, then a graded annotated bibliography, then draft, revision, and final, plus the conference-style presentation. The annotated bibliography is a real milestone rather than a warm-up. Students build and annotate a substantial set of scholarly sources, on the order of eight to twelve, and use it to place their own work in the scholarly conversation.

Two things go deeper there than in 601. Critical evaluation, because post-tonal theory is itself evolving and contested, so students weigh competing analytical frameworks and take a position rather than collect sources that agree. And synthesis, because the paper connects analysis to historical and cultural context and enters an existing conversation, which means reading sources against each other rather than one at a time.

Every item on the exit list has been exercised independently at least once by Paper 5, most of them three or four times, always at the same one-edition-plus-two-source scale. The 602 annotated bibliography is a scale jump rather than a skill jump.
