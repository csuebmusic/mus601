# MUS 601: conventions

Read this before drafting anything at all in this repo, and defer to it.

## who owns what

Each body of material has one canonical home. Edit it there; everything else points to it.

- `README.md`: the face of the course, public-facing. Course description, meeting time and place, instructor and office hours, the syllabus link, and a pointer to each folder. Each folder's `README.md` holds that folder's live links and what each page gives, and is public-facing.
- `syllabus.html`: the full course record and the source of truth for every course fact. Schedule, per-unit bibliographies, directed questions, outcomes, the analytical papers (what they are, the method, the standard, sources, the specifications, how they are assessed), assessment and the rubric, policies, term dates, meeting time, modality.
- `outline.md`: a one-page glance, mine. Dates, unit titles, paper due dates. Derived from the syllabus; keep it in step.
- `research/source-work.html`: the source and citation handout. The research tools and how to search them, what each paper asks for in sources, and the Chicago notes-bibliography forms. Shared with 602: author here, copy there, keep identical.
- `meta/research-and-bibliography.md`: the design behind the research work, mine. The tools with their access routes, the per-paper source plan, and the citation forms the handout draws on.
- `meta/editions.md`: the edition behind each seminar score, mine.
- `meta/visual-conventions.md`: the house style. Shared with 602 and with mus312.
- `meta/assessment-scheme.md`: the 0-4 grade-point scheme and the grading mechanics, reusable across courses.
- `units/unit-1.html` to `units/unit-4.html`: the per-unit pages linked from the Canvas modules. Each gives its unit's selected bibliography, weeks, works, and paper prompt with directed questions. Derived from `syllabus.html`; keep in step, and edit the syllabus first.
- `lesson-plans/`: mine. `unit-N-readings.md` is the reading synthesis for that unit: what each assigned reading argues, where the readings meet the unit's repertoire, and the open items. `unit-1-modal-theory-1000-1600.md` is the dated through-line from the eleventh-century apparatus to 1600. `unit-N-class-notes.md` is the lecture in the order of the handwritten class notes, expanded with the readings; where the class notes and a reading disagree, the reading stands. `unit-N-recap.md` is the lesson plan for that unit's paper meeting: the contextual intro and the system and form charts for the paper's works. Unit 1 files exist; the others are written as the units are built.
- `tools/`: interactive analytical tools and handouts. `tools/modal-species.html` gives the four categories of mode with the species of fourth and fifth and the authentic and plagal ambitus. `tools/gamut-layers.html` lays the hexachordal, tetrachordal, and modal divisions over one pitch field (gamut) as eleventh-century scalar and modal theory. `tools/affinities.html` gives the affinities, the cofinals, and transposition. `tools/systems.html` gives the three-hexachord gamut system, `tools/twelve-modes.html` the twelve modes under both numberings. The five are numbered 1 to 5 in their titles and carry a rail listing all five. `tools/tuning.html` gives seven keyboard tunings from 1482 to 1691 on one circle of fifths, with interval and triad sizes and playback; it is unnumbered and carries no rail.
- `meta/`: planning and conventions, mine and Claude only. Markdown anywhere in the repo, including in `lesson-plans/`, is mine and Claude's, except the READMEs. The repo is public, so all of it is readable at the repo and at the Pages URL.

## format by audience

The narrative and style rules for every document in this repo are the "how I want you to communicate" section of my Claude profile preferences, under Settings, Profile. They are not a file in this repo and are not duplicated here. Read them there and follow them.

## paper feedback

Feedback on each student's paper is one Claude Docs doc, titled "Paper N feedback, First Last." Under the title are the date, my name, and one line naming the paper and its works. The feedback is written in three passes, each its own lowercase section, and printed. Nothing from student work is committed to this repo.

### pass 1: references

The section is headed "references." It covers citation placement, note form, and the bibliography. It leaves out the specifications, what each secondary source is used for, and where an edition was found.

- Overcitation first. A note that cites the score for a measure number the sentence already gives is cut: each edition is cited once, in a full note at its first mention, and measure numbers stay in the prose. Each translation is credited once, in a note at its first quotation. Give the count and one example from the paper.
- Note form next, with note numbers: first notes set as bibliography entries, short forms, title styling, volume and issue, empty or stray notes.
- One of the student's own sources as a model, in three forms: first note, short note, bibliography entry.
- The bibliography, corrected in full and alphabetized. An item only the source can supply is left as a bracketed blank, named in one sentence above the list.
- Anything unverified stays out of the doc and is reported to me in chat.
